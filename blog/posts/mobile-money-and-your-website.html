Why Mobile Money changed what a website needs to do.

A "contact us" page used to be enough. Today, if you sell anything, visitors expect to see how they can pay before they decide to trust you, and in Ghana and much of West Africa that increasingly means Mobile Money, not just card.

21 July 2026 6 min read KoomBei

A few years ago, a small business website could get away with a "contact us for pricing" page and a phone number. That is no longer enough. If you are selling anything, a visitor now expects to see, before they decide to trust you, roughly how much it costs and how they can actually pay for it. And in Ghana, and much of West Africa, how people pay for everyday things is increasingly Mobile Money, not a bank card.

Why this matters now

For a large share of people across Ghana and the wider region, Mobile Money is not an alternative payment method, it is the default one. It is how people pay a landlord, a market seller, a school fee, and increasingly, a small business online. Card ownership is far less universal. A site that only offers card payment is quietly excluding a large part of its potential customers, not because of price or interest, but because the option they would actually use is not there.

This is not really a new trend so much as a fact about the market that a lot of websites, especially ones built with templates designed for other regions, have not caught up to.

What this means for your website in practice

  • Show the payment methods before checkout, not as a surprise at the last step. If someone has to reach the final page before discovering you do not accept Mobile Money, you have already lost the sale, and the visit.
  • Whatever payment method you offer has to work well on a mobile browser. Mobile Money users are, by definition, on their phones. A payment flow that only feels finished on a desktop screen is a payment flow most of your customers will struggle with.
  • Do not assume card-only is enough because it is simpler to set up. It is simpler for you, not for a large share of your customers.
  • Keep the whole page light. Mobile Money users are frequently on the same mobile data connections we cover in building for the connection your audience actually has. A slow payment page loses sales the same way a slow homepage loses visitors.

The one-line version

If people already pay their landlord and their market seller with Mobile Money, they will expect the option on your site too.

What we built for this

Our own RuboLink case study covers this directly. RuboLink is a marketplace connecting Ghanaian homes and businesses with vetted local professionals, and Mobile Money alongside card payment was never an optional extra, it was a requirement from the first conversation about the product. A marketplace that only accepted cards would have been asking a large share of its own customers to work around it just to pay for a plumber.

If you are not ready for payments yet

Not every site needs a payment gateway today, and adding one because it is trendy, before you have a real sales process behind it, does not create that process. If you are not yet taking orders in a structured way, get that right first. A payment gateway bolted onto a page with no clear ordering flow behind it usually creates more confusion than it solves. Add it when you have a real flow of transactions to route through it, and when you do, make Mobile Money part of the plan from day one rather than an afterthought.
